MEDICONNECT
System Overview and Pitch Brief

Prepared for presentation: November 20, 2026


1. THE SHORT VERSION

MediConnect is a healthcare coordination platform that brings together
staffing workflows, hospital-reported resource information, and patient care
requests. Patients can discover nearby hospitals, review reported resource
availability, request appointments, and submit transport requests. Hospitals
and healthcare professionals use role-specific workspaces for staffing and
operational information, with a web dashboard backed by a role-aware API.

The central idea is simple: hospitals should not have to coordinate urgent
staffing needs, critical resource updates, and transport requests through
disconnected spreadsheets, calls, and messages. MediConnect creates a
structured workflow for these tasks, so the right people can see relevant
information, take an authorized action, and follow what happened.

Suggested one-line pitch:
"MediConnect helps patients and healthcare teams coordinate care requests,
staffing, and hospital resources through one role-based platform."


2. THE PROBLEM

Healthcare operations depend on timely coordination, but key information is
often spread across separate tools and communication channels:

- A hospital may need a clinician for a particular shift, specialty, or level
  of urgency, while qualified professionals may not know where the opportunity
  is or how to respond.
- Staffing teams need to review candidates, confirm assignments, and keep a
  record of who is expected to work and when.
- Hospital teams need visibility into operational resources such as beds,
  blood stock, and oxygen supplies. Updates that are difficult to find or
  reconcile can make coordination harder.
- Patients and hospital dispatch teams need a clear way to submit and track a
  transport request, including its status and dispatcher-provided ETA.
- Manual processes can make it harder to maintain a shared, up-to-date view
  and to understand who changed a resource count or request.

MediConnect is designed to address this coordination problem. It is an
operational coordination tool, not a replacement for clinical judgment,
hospital information systems, emergency services, or clinical care.


3. WHO IT SERVES

- Hospitals and hospital administrators: publish staffing opportunities,
  review bids, manage hospital teams, and monitor operational resource records.
- Doctors, nurses, and other healthcare professionals: maintain a professional
  profile, discover open contracts, bid for work, and track assignments.
- Hospital dispatch coordinators: receive transport requests for their
  destination hospital and update dispatch, location, ETA, and status.
- Patients: discover nearby hospitals, review facility-reported resources,
  request an appointment with an available professional, and submit a
  transport request to a selected hospital.
- Platform administrators: manage the overall service and its accounts.

Access to key actions is associated with authenticated accounts and roles.
Hospital memberships identify the hospital teams allowed to manage hospital-
specific workflows.


4. WHAT THE SYSTEM DOES

A. Staffing marketplace

Hospitals can create staffing contracts for shifts, short-term cover,
longer-term or locum work, on-call work, and full-time positions. A contract
can include the required professional role, specialty, experience, schedule,
location, urgency, budget, and other requirements.

The typical workflow is:
1. A hospital administrator drafts a contract and publishes it.
2. Eligible professionals browse open public opportunities and submit a bid
   with a proposed rate and message.
3. Hospital administrators review incoming bids and accept or reject them.
4. Accepting a bid creates an assignment and closes the other pending bids for
   that contract.
5. The professional checks in and checks out; the assignment can then be
   completed and reviewed.
6. Notifications inform relevant users about bids and decisions.

B. Hospital resource visibility and management

The system provides a configurable resource catalog and hospital-level records
for resources such as beds, medications, equipment, blood, and oxygen. Resource
records can include capacity, available quantity, reserved quantity, unit,
department, low-stock threshold, and the time and user of the latest update.

Authorized hospital users can record additions, removals, reservations,
releases, or corrections. A resource adjustment records the previous and new
available quantities, who made the change, a reason, and an optional
reference. Low or critical levels can trigger in-app alerts for relevant
hospital managers.

Blood records can describe blood group, component, batch, and expiry.
Oxygen records can describe supply type, capacity, current level, number of
units, and available units. These records support operational visibility;
they are only as current and accurate as the data entered or connected by the
hospital.

C. Ambulance request workflow

An authenticated user can request transport to a destination hospital and
provide a pickup address and coordinates. Authorized staff associated with
that hospital can dispatch the request, provide an ambulance identifier,
location and ETA, then update location and progress. The request moves through
statuses such as requested, en route, arrived, completed, or cancelled.
Patients receive in-app status notifications.

The frontend includes a hospital map and route experience. Driving-route
estimates depend on a configured Google Maps key and enabled Maps services.
Without that configuration, the page can provide a direct-distance estimate
and an external map link.

D. Account, notifications, and operational dashboards

Users can sign in with email and password, maintain a profile, change a
password, and (where appropriate) submit professional verification documents.
The application includes an in-app notification inbox and user preferences.

The public landing page explains the system's capabilities and provides
sign-in and account-creation paths. Once authenticated, users are routed to
dashboards tailored to their roles. The patient dashboard brings together
notifications, nearby hospital listings, appointment booking, ambulance
requests, profile settings, and reported resource availability for oxygen,
blood, beds, and equipment. Resource lists can be searched and filtered by
hospital or type, and ranked by reported availability. Selecting a hospital
offers appointment and ambulance request actions for that facility.

The operational dashboards are also role-specific and API-backed. Platform
administrators see facility and service-level indicators; hospital
administrators see facility staffing, bids, referrals, assignments, transport,
and resource alerts; doctors and specialists see open opportunities,
appointment requests, assignments, and credential status; nurses focus on
triage referrals, appointment requests, assignments, and clinical resources;
and hospital staff see facility operations relevant to their access. Each
workspace brings priority work, recent notifications, and task shortcuts into
one view. Metrics distinguish loading or unavailable data from a reported
zero. The shared navigation groups tools by task, adapts to mobile screens,
and provides account sign-out.

Patient appointment requests record the patient's name and at least one
contact method (email or phone), along with the selected professional,
requested date/time, and duration. A hospital can optionally be selected; in
that case, the professional must have an active staff membership at the
selected hospital. Patients can view their own requests and their current
status.

The backend also exposes authenticated user activity history and a read-only,
append-only activity ledger. Users can review their own activity, while
administrators can review activity across users. These activity records
complement resource-specific transaction history; they are not clinical
records.

Operational tools include oxygen monitoring, general resource management
(including resources classified as ICU), resource transaction history,
analytics, reports, staffing, notifications, hospital maps, ambulance
requests, and account settings. There is no separate ICU dashboard.
Interfacility referrals let source-hospital nurses and authorized staff submit
a structured triage summary; a receiving-facility manager can accept, reserve
a matching resource, and create a linked ambulance request.

E. Care coordination and integration foundation

The MVP includes a driver PWA flow based on a single-use link. When a dispatch
is en route, the driver can open the link and share browser GPS over a
WebSocket. The current location is published through Redis with a five-minute
expiry and is not retained as route history. The patient and authorized source
and receiving facility members can view the live position.

Facilities can be enriched with Master Facility List codes, levels 1-6,
ownership, county/sub-county, and rankings using a validated CSV import
command. Professional document uploads enqueue a verification job, but the
KMPDC adapter deliberately stays pending until authorized provider access is
available; it does not simulate a verified result.

The service worker caches the app shell and public facility/resource snapshots.
Authorized users can queue non-clinical resource quantity changes with an
idempotency key and stale-data conflict check. Patient data and clinical triage
are excluded from the offline cache. Normalized SMS and USSD callback
interfaces support structured bed and oxygen count updates from an
authorized facility manager's registered phone. Provider-specific carrier
connections still need configuration. Hourly resource snapshots support
burn-rate estimates after sufficient observation history. M-Pesa settings
establish a configuration boundary only; checkout and money movement are
disabled.


5. HOW IT WORKS

1. A visitor opens the public MediConnect landing page in a browser-based
   React and TypeScript frontend. After sign-in, the user is directed to a
   role-specific dashboard with task shortcuts and operational indicators
   drawn from endpoints available to that role.
2. The frontend calls the Django REST API over HTTP. The API is divided into
   modules for accounts, hospitals, contracts, resources, notifications, and
   ambulance requests.
3. Users authenticate using JSON Web Tokens (JWT). The frontend holds tokens
   in memory during the session and can refresh an expired access token.
4. The backend applies authentication, role checks, and hospital-membership
   checks to protected operations. For example, a professional can bid on a
   contract, while a hospital administrator can publish it or review bids.
5. Django persists users, hospitals, staff memberships, contracts, bids,
   assignments, resource records, transactions, notifications, and ambulance
   requests in a relational database.
6. Backend endpoints expose paginated, structured data to the frontend. The
   API schema is documented with OpenAPI and can be explored through Swagger
   UI or ReDoc.

Local development can use SQLite or the included Compose stack with
PostgreSQL/TimescaleDB, Redis, Daphne, and Celery. Hourly snapshots support
resource depletion estimates. Driver locations can be streamed through the
browser-based telemetry flow, but they are short-lived and are not a
continuous fleet feed. Ambulance provider integrations are not present.


6. WHAT MAKES THE APPROACH VALUABLE

- One operational workspace: staffing, selected resource records, alerts, and
  transport requests are accessible through the same product.
- Structured workflows: contracts, bids, assignments, resource adjustments,
  and transport statuses have defined states and permitted actions.
- Accountability: resource adjustments retain a transaction record with the
  user, quantities, timestamp, and reason.
- Role-aware collaboration: users see and manage workflows relevant to their
  role and hospital membership. Dashboards surface actionable work queues,
  recent notifications, and task shortcuts without replacing backend access
  controls.
- Patient-facing coordination: patients can find nearby hospital listings,
  review reported resources, and submit appointment or transport requests.
- Interfacility handoffs: structured triage, a receiving-facility decision,
  resource reservation, and linked transport request share one workflow.
- Resilient update path: public facility data can be cached and authorized
  non-clinical resource changes can be queued for conflict-checked sync.
- Read-only activity visibility: authenticated users can inspect their own
  history, with broader ledger access available to administrators.
- Configurable resource model: resource categories and types can represent
  different hospital inventories instead of hard-coding every resource into a
  separate interface.
- Extensible API: a documented REST API offers a foundation for future
  integrations with hospital systems and other approved services.
- Clear operational focus: the system organizes coordination tasks and
  visibility; it does not claim to make clinical decisions.


7. CURRENT PRODUCT STATUS AND IMPORTANT LIMITS

The current codebase contains a functional web application and Django REST
backend for the workflows described above. The shared workspace has a
healthcare-oriented visual refresh, responsive task-grouped navigation, and
distinct operational dashboards for each account role. It is best presented
as a prototype or early-stage product pending deployment and validation with
participating hospitals.

Be precise in the pitch:

- The public landing page includes an illustrative resource overview, not live
  hospital measurements. Some disconnected frontend views can run with sample
  data, clearly labeled "Sample data - not live." Signed-in dashboard
  indicators use the configured API; facility measurements still depend on
  accurate and timely hospital updates.
- Resource levels are not automatically sensed. The application stores
  operational values supplied through its API; updates may be entered
  manually unless an integration is later built.
- The ambulance workflow is internal to the platform. It is not connected to
  an ambulance provider or emergency call center. Dispatchers manage request
  status and ETA; a driver can share browser GPS through a single-use link.
  This must not be presented as a substitute for contacting local emergency
  services.
- In-app notifications are available. SMS delivery requires the configured
  gateway; email and push delivery are not configured by default.
- Hourly resource snapshots provide a basic burn-rate estimate after at least
  one hour of history. This is not a predictive model, and reports do not
  represent a complete system-wide audit analysis.
- Driver GPS sharing requires an en-route dispatch, a single-use link, browser
  location permission, and network access. The workflow is not connected to
  fleet hardware or external ambulance dispatch systems; dispatchers must
  still coordinate through their operations team.
- KMPDC verification remains pending until authorized API access is supplied.
  Facility registry data is currently loaded through validated CSV import,
  rather than a live master-registry connection.
- SMS/USSD callbacks and outbound SMS are provider-neutral integration
  boundaries. Live delivery requires provider adapters and credentials.
- Offline mode caches only public facility/resource data and queues
  non-clinical resource updates; it does not cache triage or patient records.
- M-Pesa checkout and money movement are disabled. Configuration variables do
  not mean that payments are integrated.
- Patient-facing hospital and resource information depends on facility data
  and updates. Nearby distance is an estimate based on listed locations and,
  when the patient opts in, device location; it does not guarantee resource
  availability or clinical suitability.
- The current audit screen retrieves up to 50 transactions for a selected
  resource, rather than a complete cross-hospital audit log.
- The Organs category is a catalog/resource classification only. The system
  does not verify donor or organ availability.
- The product is not an electronic health record, clinical decision-support
  system, or validated emergency dispatch service.
- Production readiness requires deployment configuration, security review,
  reliable hosting and backups, operational monitoring, data governance, and
  validation against applicable healthcare privacy and regulatory
  requirements.

These are appropriate next steps for a pilot and product roadmap, not claims
that the current prototype already provides those integrations or assurances.


8. A PRACTICAL PILOT PLAN

1. Select one or two hospital partners and agree on a narrow initial scope.
2. Validate staff roles, resource categories, update responsibilities, and
   access policies with hospital operations teams.
3. Configure a production-grade environment, database, secrets management,
   backups, monitoring, HTTPS, and appropriate security controls.
4. Begin with a limited workflow, such as staffing contracts and resource
   status updates for a defined group of units.
5. Train designated staff and document how often operational values should be
   updated and who is accountable for them.
6. Measure operational indicators agreed with pilot partners, such as time to
   publish and fill a shift, time to update resource records, alert response
   time, and user adoption. Establish baselines before making impact claims.
7. Add integrations only after confirming technical access, data quality,
   privacy requirements, and operational ownership.


9. SUGGESTED LIVE DEMONSTRATION

Keep the demo transparent and focused:

1. Show the sign-in/connect experience and say whether you are using sample
   data or a live configured backend.
2. Show the public landing page and the role-specific workspace. Use an
   administrator, hospital manager, clinician, nurse, or staff account to
   show how metrics and priority tasks vary by access.
3. Show the patient dashboard's nearby
   hospitals and reported resource availability. Explain that the landing
   page's visual resource overview is illustrative, not live data.
4. Demonstrate submitting an appointment request with patient contact details
   and a selected professional. If choosing a hospital, explain the hospital
   affiliation requirement.
5. Open the staffing marketplace. Show a contract, a professional bid, and
   how a hospital administrator reviews it.
6. Show a resource record and demonstrate an authorized stock adjustment.
   Point out the transaction history and low-stock alert behavior.
7. Submit a demonstration interfacility referral, explain that acceptance
   reserves a resource and creates a linked dispatch request, and show the
   single-use driver PWA location flow without using real patient data.
8. Show user activity history and the read-only ledger, then reports and
   hourly burn-rate estimates. Explain that burn rates require sufficient
   snapshot history and are estimates, not predictions.

Do not display demo figures as if they were live hospital measurements, and do
not use real patient information in a public presentation.


10. PITCH SCRIPT (ABOUT ONE MINUTE)

"Hospitals coordinate care through a network of people and essential
resources, but staffing, resource updates, and patient requests can be split
across disconnected processes. MediConnect brings these workflows together
in one role-based web platform.

"Hospitals can publish staffing needs and review bids from qualified
professionals. Teams can maintain visibility into resources such as beds,
blood, and oxygen, record changes with an audit trail, and receive in-app
low-stock alerts. For hospital-to-hospital transfers, staff can share a
structured triage handoff, reserve a receiving facility resource on
acceptance, and coordinate a linked transport request. Patients can find
nearby hospital listings, review facility-reported resources, request
appointments with available professionals, and submit transport requests.

"The platform is built with a role-aware Django API and a React dashboard. Our
MVP also includes short-lived driver-browser GPS telemetry, hourly resource
snapshots, offline non-clinical resource updates, and provider-neutral SMS and
USSD boundaries. KMPDC verification remains pending until authorized access is
configured, M-Pesa payments are disabled, and no ambulance provider is
connected. We are looking for hospital partners to validate a focused pilot,
measure operational value, and guide the integrations needed for deployment."


11. THE ASK

Invite the audience to help define the next validation step:

- A pilot partner to test a clearly scoped staffing or resource workflow.
- Operational feedback from hospital administrators, staffing coordinators,
  clinicians, and dispatch teams.
- Guidance on integration requirements, data governance, privacy, and
  deployment standards.
- Agreement on measurable pilot outcomes and the baseline needed to evaluate
  them.

The goal is to move from a working prototype to a trusted, validated
operational platform through real-world collaboration.


TECHNICAL REFERENCE

Frontend: React 18, TypeScript, Vite.
Backend: Django and Django REST Framework.
Authentication: JWT-based sign-in and refresh.
API documentation: OpenAPI, Swagger UI, and ReDoc.
Development database: SQLite or Compose PostgreSQL/TimescaleDB + Redis.
Background jobs: Celery.
External map features: optional Google Maps configuration.
Development demo data: synthetic records; `manage.py seed_mvp_demo_data`
requires the core sample facilities and refuses to run when `DEBUG` is off.
